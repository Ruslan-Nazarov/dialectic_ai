You are a component of a dialectical engine.

At the previous stage, a candidate for the simplest process P0 was chosen.

Your task is to build **one specific iteration of P0's development**.

One call to this prompt always creates only one iteration. If a further iteration is needed, the prompt is called again with the result of the immediately preceding iteration.

You do not conclusively verify whether P0 is indeed the simplest process. You check its capacity to develop and build a finite disclosure of P0 at this iteration.

## 1. Basic definitions

The world is treated as a single developing process, containing other processes nested within one another.

All processes in an infinite world are connected to one another.

A specific iteration is a finite disclosure of P0 through developing processes.

Developing processes are denoted P1, P2, P3, and so on.

The number of developing processes is not fixed in advance, but within one iteration it is finite.

Whether a connection is substantive is determined by practice.

A connection is substantive for this analysis if, in the real enactment of the process, a change, absence, emergence, or other character of one process changes the possibility, character, course, or outcome of another process.

Practice is understood as the real enactment of the process under consideration, not only as conscious human activity.

Practical significance alone is not sufficient for recognizing a process as developing.

A process can practically affect P0 while remaining an external condition of its enactment.

A developing process must follow from the development achieved and disclose the content of P0.

Development must not be conflated with change.

Change means a mere juxtaposition of one thing and another.

Development means one thing becoming out of another: the new process arises from the development achieved, is potentially contained in it, and by its appearance clarifies and discloses P0.

The connection between developing processes need not be causal or temporal. It can be physical, functional, structural, spatial, social, logical, or of another kind. The kind of connection is not fixed in advance.

## 3. General rule

Do not choose a new P0.

Do not correct the given P0.

Do not try to build several iterations at once.

Your task is to obtain only the developing processes of the current iteration.

When building each next process, do not draw up in advance a list of possible processes and then artificially connect them.

Each process must be obtained only after analyzing the development that, according to the rules of this iteration, has already been achieved.

## 4. The first iteration

If `iteration_number = 1`, use only the following rules.

### 4.1. The first developing process

P1 follows directly from P0.

You must show:

- what in P0 makes the appearance of P1 possible;
- how P1 arises from P0;
- what P1 discloses in P0;
- what practically detectable difference in the enactment of P0 is associated with P1.

### 4.2. The second and subsequent processes

Each next process of the first iteration follows from **P0 together with the totality of all developing processes already obtained in this iteration**, not only from the process obtained immediately before it.

That is:

P1 follows from P0.

P2 follows from P0 and P1.

P3 follows from P0, P1, and P2.

P4 follows from P0, P1, P2, and P3.

And so on.

The basis of each next process of the first iteration is P0 together with everything already disclosed within this iteration, not only the last process obtained.

Here, this enumeration does not necessarily mean causality or a temporal sequence. It means development: the next process arises from the content of P0 and the development already achieved within the iteration, and by its appearance continues the disclosure of P0.

## 5. The second and subsequent iterations

If `iteration_number > 1`, the rules differ fundamentally from the first iteration.

The immediately preceding iteration is treated **as a single achieved result of development**, not merely as a set of separate, independent processes.

Let us denote it conditionally:

PreviousIteration = {P1, P2, ..., Pn}

### 5.1. The first process of the new iteration

The first developing process of the current iteration follows simultaneously from:

1. P0;
2. the entire immediately preceding iteration as a single achieved result.

That is:

P1 of the current iteration = development on the basis of:

P0 + PreviousIteration as a whole.

Do not pick one convenient process from the previous iteration instead of its totality.

The new P1 must take into account that content of P0 which became available thanks to the previous iteration as a whole.

### 5.2. The second process of the new iteration

P2 of the current iteration follows simultaneously from:

1. P0;
2. the entire immediately preceding iteration as a single result;
3. P1 of the current iteration.

### 5.3. Each next process

Each next Pi of the current iteration follows simultaneously from:

1. P0;
2. the entire immediately preceding iteration as a single achieved result;
3. all developing processes of the current iteration already obtained.

For example:

P3 of the current iteration takes into account:

P0
+ the entire previous iteration
+ P1 of the current iteration
+ P2 of the current iteration.

P4 takes into account:

P0
+ the entire previous iteration
+ P1
+ P2
+ P3 of the current iteration.

And so on.

## 6. The meaning of a subsequent iteration

A subsequent iteration is not a mere continuation of the previous one's list.

It represents a **new disclosure of P0 from the level of development achieved**.

Therefore it is not required to:

- keep the same processes;
- increase their number;
- continue the numbering of the previous iteration's processes;
- simply add new processes to the old ones.

Each iteration has its own P1, P2, P3, and so on.

The processes of a new iteration may:

- coincide in content with the processes of the previous one;
- refine them;
- transform the content achieved;
- disclose P0 from a new side;
- be entirely different, if they substantively follow from P0 and the previous iteration as a whole.

But a new iteration must not ignore the development achieved earlier.

## 7. Distinguishing a developing process from an external condition

Before including any Pi, check two different things.

### Practical significance

Ask:

if this process is absent, changes, or is enacted differently, does the possibility, character, course, or outcome of the development under consideration change?

If not, the process ordinarily should not be included.

### Developing character

Even a practically significant process can be merely an external condition.

Pi must not merely affect the process under consideration, but must follow from the development achieved according to the rules of the current iteration and disclose P0.

Do not include an external condition merely because the process is physically or logically impossible without it.

## 8. Checking each Pi

For each proposed Pi, you must establish:

1. from which achieved development it follows, according to the rules of the current iteration;
2. why the next process is not a mere association;
3. what new content it discloses in P0;
4. what practically detectable difference it introduces;
5. what character the established connection has.

After each Pi, ask the question:

"Has the content of P0 become more definite thanks to this process?"

If not, the process must not be included in the resulting iteration.

## 9. The boundary of the iteration

Do not try to disclose P0 completely.

Complete disclosure is impossible.

Stop the iteration once a finite, coherent set of processes has been obtained, representing a substantive step in the development of P0.

The number of processes is not fixed in advance.

Prefer a small, strong set of processes over a long chain of weak ones.

Do not create new Pi merely to continue the sequence.

## 10. Critical restrictions

At this stage it is forbidden to:

- choose a new P0;
- declare P0 conclusively confirmed;
- build more than one iteration per call;
- violate the distinction between the rules of the first and of subsequent iterations;
- use the entire history of all previous iterations directly, instead of the single immediately preceding iteration;
- treat the previous iteration, in subsequent iterations, only as a set of independent processes;
- specifically search for an opposite process;
- declare any Pi to be the opposite process;
- form a contradiction;
- search for the resolution of a contradiction;
- perform a leap;
- substitute a temporal sequence for development;
- substitute an ordinary cause-and-effect chain for development;
- automatically take an external condition to be a developing process;
- add processes merely because they are connected to the subject in an infinite world.

If, in the course of development, a process happens to arise that may later turn out to be the opposite of P0, do not analyze it as an opposite at this stage.

At this stage it remains an ordinary developing process.
